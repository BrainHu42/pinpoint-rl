Hedging when unsure: claim C3, explained

When the system isn't sure of the exact place, it gives a safe answer in the middle of the likely region. That makes it right more often at region and country scale, without hurting city scale.

1. How a guess is scored

A guess is checked against five distances from the true spot. Each column of the results table is the share of photos whose guess lands inside that ring.

2. The problem: only one column ever moved

Everything built before was trained to maximise the 25 km column. Against the released Pinpoint on the test set, our pipeline gained there and nowhere else: at country and continent scale it tied.

Why: when unsure, the old system still named one specific town, an all-or-nothing bet. Its wrong towns were usually in another region (median 381 km off), so a miss at 25 km was often a miss at 750 km too.

3. The idea: price every answer at every distance

The value network estimates, for each possible answer, the chance of landing within each ring: P(< 1 km), P(< 25 km) and so on. Besides specific towns it may answer with a hedge, the centre of the likely region. Then it picks the answer with the best expected score:

score = w1·P(<1) + P(<25) + wcoarse·[P(<200) + P(<750) + P(<2500)]

  • The gain: +2.9 to +4.7 at region (200 km) and country (750 km) scale.
  • City scale unchanged: the hedged photos were mostly ones it would have missed at 25 km anyway.
  • The cost: 1 km. Some photos where a bold exact guess would have hit within 1 km get the vaguer answer.
  • It's the hedges: the same weights without hedge answers give only +0.5 / +0.9 at 750 km.

What this does and doesn't show

  • Dev sets only; the test table needs a separate go-ahead.
  • The probabilities are honest on dev3k (within 0.03-0.04 of what happens) but overconfident on commons26 (trained on MP16 only).
  • The gain comes from a decision rule (best expected score), not a better image model, which is why it stacks with other improvements.
